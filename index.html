Subway Navigation & NYC Travel Hacks

Essential local wisdom, subway rules, airport transit hacks, tipping standards, and clean restroom locations to help you navigate Manhattan and Brooklyn seamlessly.

Subway Navigation: Express vs. Local

The New York City Subway carries 4 million passengers daily across 472 stations. Understanding three core principles will save hours of lost time:
  • Express vs. Local Trains: On MTA maps, white circular dots indicate express stations; black dots indicate local-only stations. On the tracks: Local trains stop at every station along the line; Express trains skip minor stops to barrel between major hubs. For example, on the Lexington Avenue line, the 6 is Local while the 4 and 5 are Express. If traveling to an intermediate local stop, ensure you board the local train!
  • Uptown vs. Downtown Direction: In Manhattan, "Uptown" means heading North (toward Central Park, Harlem, and higher street numbers). "Downtown" means heading South (toward Soho, Wall Street, and lower street numbers). Many historic stations have separate street stairs for Uptown and Downtown with NO crossover tunnel underneath—always check the street hood sign before entering turnstiles!
  • OMNY Contactless Fare & $34 Cap: Tap any contactless bank card, Apple Pay, or Google Wallet. The flat fare is $2.90. After 12 taps with the exact same device between Monday morning and Sunday midnight ($34.80 total), all subsequent rides that week are automatically 100% free. Free 2-hour transfers between subway lines or between subway and city buses happen automatically.
  • The Empty Subway Car Rule: If a subway train pulls in and every car is packed with commuters except for one suspiciously empty car, do not enter it. In New York, an empty car always has a reason—usually a malfunctioning air conditioner in summer or an intolerable odor.

Manhattan Street Rules & Pedestrian Etiquette

New York is a walking city with an unspoken social contract:
  • Escalator Discipline: Stand on the right; walk on the left. If you stand on the left side of an escalator in an MTA station, commuters will briskly tap your shoulder.
  • The Sidewalk Pull-Over: Never abruptly stop in the middle of a sidewalk to check your phone or consult Google Maps. Step aside toward a building wall or street pole to maintain pedestrian traffic flow.
  • Jaywalking Culture: New Yorkers cross streets whenever there is a natural gap in vehicular traffic, regardless of signal light color. However, be cautious of silent electric delivery scooters traveling in dedicated bike lanes.
  • Avenues vs. Streets Distance: Streets run East-West; Avenues run North-South. Exactly 20 street blocks equal 1 mile (~1.6 km), taking roughly 20 minutes to walk. Avenue blocks are three times longer (about 7 avenue blocks equal 1 mile).

Airport Transit: JFK, Newark & LaGuardia

  • JFK to Midtown Manhattan: Take the JFK AirTrain to Jamaica Station ($8.50), then transfer to the Long Island Rail Road (LIRR) train directly into Grand Central Madison or Penn Station ($7.00 off-peak / $10.75 peak). The entire trip takes only 35 minutes and avoids expressway traffic jams. Yellow taxis charge a flat regulated rate of $70.00 plus tolls ($7–$10) and tip.
  • Newark (EWR) to Manhattan: Take the AirTrain to Newark Airport Station, then catch NJ Transit rail into New York Penn Station (30 minutes, $15.75 total).
  • LaGuardia (LGA) to Manhattan: Take the free LaGuardia Link Q70-SBS bus to the Jackson Heights–Roosevelt Ave subway station (connecting to E, F, M, R, 7 trains). Fast, reliable, and costs only a standard $2.90 subway tap.

Hailing Yellow Cabs vs. Ride-Hailing

  • How to Hail a Yellow Cab: Stand at the street curb and raise your arm. Look at the rooftop numbers: if the rooftop light is illuminated, the taxi is vacant and available. If the roof light is dark, it is occupied. If the light says 'Off Duty', the driver will not stop.
  • The Curb App: Download the Curb mobile app—it allows you to e-hail official yellow and green taxis with metered rates, avoiding surge pricing during rainy rush hours.

New York City Tipping Norms

In the United States, service staff rely on gratuities for the majority of their income:
  • Sit-Down Restaurants: 18% (standard good service), 20% (great service), 22% (exceptional service). Always verify the printed bill to check if 'Gratuity' or 'Service Charge' is already included, especially for parties of 5 or more.
  • Cocktail Bars & Pubs: $1.00 per beer or glass of wine; $2.00 to $3.00 per craft cocktail.
  • Yellow Taxis & Rideshare: 15% to 20% of the total metered fare.
  • Hotel Bellhops & Housekeeping: $2.00 to $5.00 per bag; $3.00 to $5.00 per day for room attendants.

Clean Public Restroom Locations in Manhattan

Clean restrooms can be notoriously scarce in New York. Keep these reliable public sanctuaries bookmarked:
  • Bryant Park Public Restroom (42nd St & 6th Ave): Universally regarded as the cleanest public restroom in America, featuring fresh flower bouquets, classical music, and dedicated attendants.
  • New York Public Library (Main Branch, 42nd St): Clean, marble ground-floor restrooms accessible during opening hours.
  • Grand Central Terminal: Large municipal restrooms located on the lower dining concourse near the track entrances.
  • The Oculus / Brookfield Place (Downtown): Sparkling modern facilities on the concourse levels of the World Trade Center transit complex.
  • Chelsea Market: Clean restrooms located in the middle corridor near the waterfall feature.
